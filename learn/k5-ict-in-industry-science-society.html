ICT in Industry, Science & Society

Factories, research, transport, law and more

🏭 Industry

Industrial simulation models a system, such as a new production line or a nuclear plant, on a computer so designers can test it before building. Process control uses sensors to monitor a continuous process, such as temperature in a brewery or pressure in an oil refinery; the computer compares readings with set values and adjusts valves or heaters automatically. Computer-aided design (CAD) lets engineers and architects draw and test products and buildings on screen, and computer-aided manufacturing (CAM) uses those designs to control machines that cut and shape parts. Robots do repetitive, heavy or dangerous jobs, such as welding and painting car bodies, accurately and without tiring.

🔬 Science, research and libraries

Weather forecasting uses powerful computers to process huge amounts of data from weather stations, satellites and radar, run models and predict the weather. In medical research computers analyse test results, model diseases and help design drugs; in hospitals expert systems help diagnose illness. The military uses ICT for simulating battles, guiding missiles and keeping secure communication. Library systems record the lending of books (who borrowed what and when it is due), keep an inventory of stock, and hold a computerised catalogue so users can search by title, author or subject.

🚆 Transport, entertainment and the home

Air traffic control uses radar and computers to track aircraft and keep them safely apart during take-off, flight and landing. Shipping and railway control systems track vessels and trains and control signals and points. GPS (global positioning system) uses satellites to find the exact position of a vehicle, so companies can track lorries, buses and parcels. In entertainment, computers are used to play and create games, record and edit music, and edit video and make special effects. At home people use ICT for study, budgeting, communication and entertainment, and to control appliances.

🛡️ Expert systems, marketing, VR and law enforcement

An expert system copies the knowledge of a human expert. It has three parts: a knowledge base (facts and rules from experts), an inference engine (which applies the rules to the user's answers to reach a conclusion) and a user interface (through which the user asks and answers questions). In marketing, ICT supports e-commerce (buying and selling online), electronic presentations to clients, and advertising on websites and social media. Virtual reality (VR) creates a 3D artificial world that the user enters with a head-mounted display (HMD) and data gloves that sense hand movements. Law enforcement uses biometrics such as fingerprint matching to identify suspects, CCTV cameras to monitor public places, and computerised police records to store and search criminal records quickly.

📝 In the exam

KCSE often asks: "Explain three ways computers are used in industry", "Name the three components of an expert system and state the function of each", "Explain how ICT is used in weather forecasting", "State two devices used to interact with a virtual reality system", or "Describe how computers are used in law enforcement". Remember: process control uses sensors and works in real time; CAD is for designing, CAM is for manufacturing; an expert system is knowledge base + inference engine + user interface; VR uses a head-mounted display and data gloves. Always link the device or system to what it does.

Key points

  • Process control uses sensors to monitor a process and adjusts it automatically.
  • CAD is used to design products; CAM uses computers to control the machines that make them.
  • Robots do repetitive, heavy or dangerous work, like welding car bodies.
  • Weather forecasting uses supercomputers to process data from satellites and weather stations.
  • An expert system has a knowledge base, an inference engine and a user interface.
  • Virtual reality uses a head-mounted display and data gloves.
  • Police use fingerprint matching, CCTV and computerised records to fight crime.

Try it for real: Spot ICT around you

  • Look out for CCTV cameras on a street or in a shop, and note what they might help with.
  • Listen to a weather forecast on radio or TV and note which information it gives, such as rain or temperature.
  • Ask someone who has used a GPS map app how it knew where they were.
