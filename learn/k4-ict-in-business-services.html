🏦 Financial systems

Businesses use accounting packages to record sales, purchases, invoices and ledgers, and payroll systems to calculate each worker's gross pay, deductions (such as PAYE and NSSF) and net pay, then print payslips. Banks use ICT everywhere: automated teller machines (ATMs) let customers withdraw cash and check balances at any time using a card and PIN; cheque clearing uses magnetic ink character recognition (MICR) to read the cheque number, bank code and account number printed in magnetic ink quickly and accurately; and internet and mobile banking let customers transfer money and pay bills without visiting a branch. M-Pesa is a form of electronic funds transfer (EFT): money moves from one account to another electronically, with no cash or cheque changing hands.

🛒 Retail systems

Supermarkets use point-of-sale (POS) terminals at the till. A barcode reader scans the product's barcode, and the computer looks up the item's name and price in the stock file, prints an itemised receipt and reduces the stock level. This gives automatic stock control: when an item falls below its reorder level, the system flags it or orders more from the supplier. Customers can pay by electronic funds transfer at point of sale (EFTPOS), using a debit card or mobile money, so the amount is moved straight from the customer's account to the shop's account.

✈️ Reservation systems

Reservation systems let customers book seats or rooms in advance from a central computer, for airlines (such as Kenya Airways), hotels, long-distance buses and the SGR train. They are real-time, online systems: an agent or a customer on the internet checks availability, books, and pays, and the central database is updated immediately. To avoid double booking, the system locks a seat or room while one transaction is being processed, so no other terminal can book it at the same moment, and once sold it is shown as unavailable to everyone.

🎓 Education

Computer-aided instruction (CAI) uses the computer to teach: it presents lessons, drill-and-practice exercises and tests, and marks them, taking some of the teacher's role. Computer-aided learning (CAL) uses the computer as a resource that learners explore for themselves, such as encyclopaedias and tutorials. E-learning delivers lessons over the internet, so learners can study at a distance and at their own pace. Computer-based simulation models real situations, such as a science experiment or a flight, so learners can practise safely and cheaply.

📝 In the exam

KCSE often asks: "Explain two ways ICT is used in the banking industry", "Describe how a POS terminal helps in stock control", "Explain how a reservation system prevents double booking", or "Differentiate between CAI and CAL". Each point should name the system and say what it does, for example "ATMs allow customers to withdraw cash at any time without queueing in a banking hall". For communication, remember fax (sends a scanned copy of a document over telephone lines), email, radio and TV broadcasting, video conferencing (people in different places meet using cameras, microphones and screens over a network) and telecommuting (working from home using a computer linked to the office network).

Key points

  • Payroll systems calculate gross pay, deductions and net pay, and print payslips.
  • Banks read cheques using magnetic ink character recognition, MICR.
  • M-Pesa is an example of electronic funds transfer: money moves electronically, with no cash.
  • A point-of-sale terminal scans barcodes, prints receipts and updates the stock level.
  • Reservation systems update a central database in real time, so a seat cannot be sold twice.
  • CAI teaches and tests the learner; CAL lets the learner explore and learn on their own.
  • Telecommuting means working from home using a computer linked to the office.

Try it for real: ICT in your town

  • Next time you are at a supermarket, watch the till: count how many items are scanned by barcode and note what is printed on the receipt.
  • Ask an adult how they pay bills or send money without visiting a bank. Which system do they use?
  • Look at an online bus, train or airline booking page and find where it shows which seats are already taken.
