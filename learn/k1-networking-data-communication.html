🌐 What a network is, and why we network

A computer network is a collection of independent computers and other devices linked together by a transmission medium so that they can communicate and share resources. Purposes of networking: resource sharing (data, programs and hardware such as one printer for a whole lab), remote communication (users far apart exchange messages and files, as when a bank branch in Kisumu updates records held in Nairobi), distributed processing (tasks and data are spread across several computers instead of one central machine), cost effectiveness (shared hardware and software licences cost less than buying for every user) and reliability (if one computer fails, data can be fetched from another and work continues).

⚠️ Limitations of networking

Networks also bring problems. Security threats: data is exposed to hackers, viruses and eavesdropping as it moves across the network. High initial cost: cables, devices, network software and trained staff are expensive to set up. Moral and cultural effects: easy access to the internet can expose users, especially the young, to pornography and other harmful material, and can erode cultural values. Over-reliance: when an organisation depends fully on its network, a breakdown stops all work, as when a bank's system goes down and no transactions can be made. Networks are classified by area as LAN (local area network: one building or campus such as a school), MAN (metropolitan area network: a town or city) and WAN (wide area network: a country or the world, the internet being the largest).

📶 Data communication terms

Data communication is the transmission of data from one location to another. A data signal is the electromagnetic or voltage form in which data travels. An analogue signal is a continuous wave that varies smoothly; a digital signal is discrete, made of on/off pulses (1s and 0s). Modulation converts digital data into analogue form for transmission and demodulation converts it back; a modem (modulator-demodulator) does both. Multiplexing sends several signals at the same time over one medium; a multiplexer combines them and a demultiplexer separates them at the other end. Bandwidth is the maximum amount of data a channel can carry per second, measured in bits per second (bps). Baseband carries one signal at a time using the whole bandwidth of the medium, as in most LANs; broadband carries many signals at once on different frequencies. Attenuation is the loss of signal strength as it travels further along a medium. Guided (bounded) media direct signals along a physical path such as a cable; unguided (unbounded) media send signals through air or space.

↔️ Transmission modes, serial and parallel

Simplex: data flows in one direction only, for example radio and television broadcasts such as KBC. Half duplex: data flows in both directions but only one way at a time, for example a walkie-talkie or police radio where users take turns. Full duplex: data flows in both directions at the same time, for example a telephone conversation. Serial transmission sends bits one after another along a single line; it is cheaper and reliable over long distances, so it is used in USB, network cables and telephone lines. Parallel transmission sends several bits at the same time along separate lines; it is faster but only suitable for short distances because bits can arrive out of step (skew), so it was used for old printer cables and inside the computer's buses.

📝 In the exam

KCSE often asks: define a computer network; state three purposes of networking; state two limitations of networks; differentiate between LAN and WAN; define terms such as bandwidth, attenuation, multiplexing, modulation and baseband; explain the difference between simplex, half duplex and full duplex giving an example of each; and compare serial and parallel transmission. Remember: a modem modulates (digital to analogue) and demodulates (analogue to digital); attenuation is loss of signal strength over distance; bandwidth is measured in bits per second; and always give an example with each transmission mode to earn the full marks.

Key points

  • A network is computers and devices linked by a medium so they can communicate and share resources.
  • Purposes of networking: resource sharing, remote communication, distributed processing, cost effectiveness and reliability.
  • Limitations include security threats, high initial cost, moral and cultural effects, and over-reliance on the network.
  • A modem modulates digital data into analogue signals and demodulates it back.
  • Bandwidth is the maximum data a channel carries per second; attenuation is loss of signal strength over distance.
  • Simplex is one way only, half duplex is both ways in turns, and full duplex is both ways at once.
  • Serial sends bits one after another; parallel sends several bits at the same time over short distances.

Try it for real: Spot data communication around you

  • Look at your school computer lab or a cybercafé: list three resources that are shared over the network.
  • Listen to a radio, use a phone and, if possible, watch someone use a two-way radio; decide which transmission mode each one uses.
  • Find a modem or home router and read its label: does it mention the speed or bandwidth in Mbps?
