RAM & Storage

Memory and the Disk

🎒 RAM is the backpack

RAM holds what the computer is using right now: the open app, the page you're reading. It is very fast but it empties when you switch off.

📚 Storage is the shelf

Storage (the disk) keeps everything even with the power off: your apps, documents, photos, music. SSDs are fast; older hard disks (HDD) spin a platter and are slower.

🚀 SSD vs HDD

An SSD has no moving parts and loads much faster than a spinning hard disk. The fastest type, NVMe, makes programs and files open quickly.

🐌 When RAM fills up

If RAM fills, the computer borrows slower space on the disk and everything feels sluggish. Closing unused browser tabs and apps frees the backpack.

📏 How big?

RAM is usually a few GB (e.g. 4, 8, 16). Storage is bigger — often hundreds of GB or 1 TB. More RAM helps with many apps open; more storage holds more files.

Key points

  • RAM holds what the computer is using right now — and empties when off.
  • Storage, the disk, keeps everything even with the power off.
  • An SSD has no moving parts and is much faster than an old hard disk.
  • NVMe SSDs are the fastest and open programs quickly.
  • If RAM fills up, the computer slows down.
  • Close unused tabs and apps to free the backpack.
  • RAM is a few GB; storage is often hundreds of GB or a whole terabyte.

Try it for real: Memory detective

  • In Task Manager > Performance (or a monitor), note total RAM and how much is in use.
  • Open about ten browser tabs and check RAM again. Did it rise?
  • Find whether your disk is an SSD or HDD, and its size.
