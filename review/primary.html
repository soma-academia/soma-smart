D1 · Meet the Computer

What's inside the box · CBE strand · Grade 4–6 · Digital devices & their parts · lesson page

Reading cards

🖥️ What is a computer? Card 1

A computer is a machine that follows instructions to do work: typing a letter, playing a video, storing photos. A desktop, a laptop, even the smartphone in your pocket — zote ni computers.

🧠 The brain: CPU Card 2

Inside is a chip called the CPU (processor). It is the ubongo — the brain — that does the thinking and follows your instructions, step by step, very fast.

🏗️ Memory and storage Card 3

RAM is the short-term memory — like your hand holding what you use right now. Storage (the disk) is the long-term keeper, like a cupboard that holds your files even after you switch off.

⌨️ Input and output Card 4

You put things IN with a keyboard, mouse or touch screen (input). The computer shows results OUT on the screen or speaker (output). Input, process, output — ingiza, fanya kazi, toa.

⚡ Hardware vs software Card 5

Hardware is the parts you can touch (screen, keyboard). Software is the programs inside that you cannot touch (a browser, a game, the calculator). Hardware ni kushika, software ni programu.

Narration

  • Karibu! Let's meet the computer.
  • A computer is a machine that follows instructions to do work.
  • Inside is the CPU — the ubongo, the brain that does the thinking.
  • RAM holds what you are using now; the disk stores files for keeps.
  • You put things in with a keyboard or mouse: that is input.
  • It shows results on the screen: that is output.
  • Parts you touch are hardware; programs inside are software. Sawa?

D2 · Mouse & Keyboard

Control the computer · CBE strand · Grade 4–6 · Basic operations: mouse & keyboard · lesson page

Reading cards

🖱️ Using the mouse Card 1

The mouse moves a pointer on the screen. Left-click to choose. Double-click to open. Right-click shows a menu of extra choices. Drag = hold the button and move.

⌨️ The keyboard Card 2

Keys type letters and numbers. The long bar is the Spacebar. Enter starts a new line or confirms. Backspace rubs out the letter before the cursor.

⇧ Shift and Caps Card 3

Hold Shift to type a CAPITAL letter or the symbol on top of a key. Caps Lock keeps all letters capital until you press it again. On a phone, tap the ⇧ arrow key once for one capital letter, or twice to keep capitals on.

✂️ Copy, cut, paste Card 4

Ctrl + C copies, Ctrl + X cuts (moves), Ctrl + V pastes. Ctrl + Z undoes a mistake. These shortcuts save you a lot of time — they work in school work too. On a phone: press and hold a word to select it, tap Copy, then press and hold where you want it and tap Paste.

👆 Touch screens Card 5

On a phone or tablet, your finger is the mouse. Tap to choose, press-and-hold for a menu, swipe to scroll. Same ideas, different tool.

Narration

  • Let's learn to control the computer.
  • The mouse moves a pointer. Left-click to choose, double-click to open.
  • Right-click shows a menu with more choices.
  • On the keyboard, Enter confirms and Backspace rubs out a letter.
  • Hold Shift for a CAPITAL letter.
  • Ctrl C copies, Ctrl V pastes, Ctrl Z undoes. Handy for homework!
  • On a phone, your finger does the same jobs. Easy.

Mission: Mouse and keyboard practice

  • Open any writing app (on a phone, the Notes or Messages app works). Type your name, then make it CAPITAL using Shift (the ⇧ key on a phone).
  • Type a short sentence, then use Backspace to fix one letter.
  • Copy your sentence (Ctrl+C) and paste it (Ctrl+V) on a new line. On a phone: press and hold, tap Copy, then Paste.

D3 · Files & Folders

Keep your work tidy · CBE strand · Grade 4–6 · File handling / basic operations · lesson page

Reading cards

📄 What is a file? Card 1

A file is one saved thing: a photo, a song, a homework document. Each file has a name and a type (like .jpg for a picture or .pdf for a document).

📁 Folders keep order Card 2

A folder is like a cardboard file or a drawer that holds related files together. 'School', 'Photos', 'Music' — giving folders clear names helps you find things fast.

💾 Save often Card 3

When you make something, Save it so it is not lost. 'Save As' makes a new copy with a new name — good for trying changes without spoiling the first one.

✏️ Name things clearly Card 4

'Homework_Science_Term2' is easier to find later than 'New document (3)'. A clear name today saves searching tomorrow.

☁️ Back it up Card 5

A backup is a spare copy kept somewhere safe — a flash disk or the cloud. If a phone is lost or a laptop spoils, your backup still has your work. Back up anything you'd be sad to lose.

Narration

  • Let's keep your work tidy and safe.
  • A file is one saved thing: a photo, a song, a homework document.
  • A folder holds related files together, like a drawer.
  • Give folders clear names: School, Photos, Music.
  • Always Save your work so it is not lost.
  • Save As makes a new copy to try changes safely.
  • And back up important work to a flash disk or the cloud. Usipoteze kazi yako!

Mission: Tidy up

  • Make a new folder and give it a clear name.
  • Create or move two files into it.
  • With a grown-up, copy one important file to a flash disk or cloud as a backup.

D4 · Stay Safe Online

Shields up · CBE strand · Grade 4–6 · Digital safety & responsible use · lesson page

Reading cards

🛡️ Your secret identity Card 1

Online, keep your real name, home, school and phone number private from strangers. Use a nickname/username. Only share real details with grown-ups you trust in real life.

🔑 Strong passwords Card 2

A password is the key to your account. Make it long and hard to guess, like a short silly sentence. Keep it secret — even from friends. Usimpe mtu password yako.

🎣 Spot the tricks Card 3

If someone online offers free airtime, free data, or a prize for your password or an M-Pesa PIN, it is a scam. Real companies never ask for your PIN or password. If it's too good to be true, ni uwongo.

🙅 Strangers online Card 4

If a stranger wants to chat privately, asks for photos, wants to meet, or makes you feel weird or scared — stop, don't reply, and tell a trusted grown-up. It is never your fault.

⚖️ Balance your time Card 5

Screens are useful but too much tires your eyes, spoils sleep and steals time for friends, sport and family. Agree your screen time with a grown-up. You control the screen, not the other way round.

Narration

  • This is the most important lesson: staying safe online.
  • Keep your real name, home and school private from strangers.
  • Make your password long and secret. Usimpe mtu password yako!
  • Free airtime or a prize for your PIN or password? Ni scam. Walk away.
  • If a stranger makes you feel weird, don't reply. Tell a grown-up.
  • Too much screen time tires your eyes and steals your time.
  • Agree your screen time with a grown-up. You are the boss, si simu.

Mission: Safety promise

  • With a grown-up, make one strong password and agree where to keep it safe.
  • Write down one thing you will never share with strangers online.
  • Agree your daily screen time and write it somewhere you both see.

D5 · What is the Internet?

The big network · Going further · lesson page

Reading cards

🌐 A network of computers Card 1

The internet is millions of computers all over the world joined together so they can share information. When you open a website or send a WhatsApp, you are using the internet.

📡 How your phone connects Card 2

Your phone or computer joins the internet through Wi-Fi (at home or school) or mobile data from a network like Safaricom or Airtel. That connects you to the ISP — the company that gives internet.

✉️ Messages travel in pieces Card 3

Your message is broken into small pieces called packets, like sending a long letter in several envelopes. They travel separately and join up again at the other end. Fast!

🏢 Websites live on servers Card 4

A website is kept on a powerful computer called a server that is always on. When you visit tingg.africa or a school site, your phone asks that server for the page.

🔒 The padlock means safe Card 5

When you see https and a padlock in the address bar, your connection is encrypted — scrambled so others can't read it. Look for the padlock before typing a password or paying.

Narration

  • The internet is millions of computers joined together to share information.
  • Your phone joins through Wi-Fi or mobile data from Safaricom or Airtel.
  • That connects you to your ISP — the company that provides internet.
  • Your message travels as small pieces called packets.
  • Websites live on always-on computers called servers.
  • When you visit a site, your phone asks that server for the page.
  • See https and a padlock? That means your connection is safe. Always check.

Mission: Internet explorer

  • Ask a grown-up: does your home/school use Wi-Fi or mobile data? Which network?
  • Open a website and look at the address bar — find the padlock and the https.
  • Name one thing you do that needs the internet, and one that does not.
