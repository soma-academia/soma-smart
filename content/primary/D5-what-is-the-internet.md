# D5 · What is the Internet?

## Read

### 🌐 A network of computers

The internet is millions of computers all over the world joined together so they can share information. When you open a website or send a WhatsApp, you are using the internet.

### 📡 How your phone connects

Your phone or computer joins the internet through Wi-Fi (at home or school) or mobile data from a network like Safaricom or Airtel. That connects you to the ISP (Internet Service Provider) — the company that gives internet.

### ✉️ Messages travel in pieces

Your message is broken into small pieces called packets, like sending a long letter in several envelopes. They travel separately and join up again at the other end. Fast!

### 🏢 Websites live on servers

A website is kept on a powerful computer called a server that is always on. When you visit tingg.africa or a school site, your phone asks that server for the page.

### 🔒 The padlock means safe

When you see https and a padlock in the address bar, your connection is encrypted — scrambled so others can't read it. Look for the padlock before typing a password or paying.

- [x] ISP
- [ ] CPU
- [ ] SSD
- [ ] APP
Why: An ISP (Internet Service Provider), like Safaricom or Airtel, connects your home or phone to the internet.

- [x] Wi-Fi or mobile data

Why: Wi-Fi brings internet from a nearby router through the air, and mobile data comes from your network's masts.

### Messages travel across the internet as...
- [x] Small pieces called packets
- [ ] One big block sent all at once

Why: A message is split into small packets that travel separate ways and are put back together at the end.

### A website is stored on a...
- [x] Server
- [ ] Router
- [ ] Mouse
- [ ] Printer
Why: A server is a computer that stays on day and night, sending web pages to anyone who asks for them.

- [x] Your connection is encrypted (scrambled)
- [ ] The website is free to use
- [ ] The page will load faster
- [ ] The site has no adverts
Why: The padlock means what you send is scrambled on the way, so other people can't read it.

- [x] The padlock / https

- [ ] Lots of pictures and adverts

Why: Check for the padlock and https first, so your password is scrambled while it travels to the site.
