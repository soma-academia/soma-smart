# K4 · ICT in Business & Services

## Read

### 🏦 Financial systems

Businesses use accounting packages to record sales, purchases, invoices and ledgers, and payroll systems to calculate each worker's gross pay, deductions such as PAYE (Pay As You Earn) and NSSF (National Social Security Fund), and net pay, then print payslips. Banks use ICT (Information and Communication Technology) everywhere: automated teller machines (ATMs) let customers withdraw cash and check balances at any time using a card and PIN (Personal Identification Number); cheque clearing uses magnetic ink character recognition (MICR) to read the cheque number, bank code and account number printed in magnetic ink quickly and accurately; and internet and mobile banking let customers transfer money and pay bills without visiting a branch. M-Pesa is a form of electronic funds transfer (EFT): money moves from one account to another electronically, with no cash or cheque changing hands.

### 🛒 Retail systems

Supermarkets use point-of-sale (POS) terminals at the till. A barcode reader scans the product's barcode, and the computer looks up the item's name and price in the stock file, prints an itemised receipt and reduces the stock level. This gives automatic stock control: when an item falls below its reorder level, the system flags it or orders more from the supplier. Customers can pay by electronic funds transfer at point of sale (EFTPOS), using a debit card or mobile money, so the amount is moved straight from the customer's account to the shop's account.

### ✈️ Reservation systems

Reservation systems let customers book seats or rooms in advance from a central computer, for airlines (such as Kenya Airways), hotels, long-distance buses and the SGR (Standard Gauge Railway) train. They are real-time, online systems: an agent or a customer on the internet checks availability, books, and pays, and the central database is updated immediately. To avoid double booking, the system locks a seat or room while one transaction is being processed, so no other terminal can book it at the same moment, and once sold it is shown as unavailable to everyone.

### 🎓 Education

Computer-aided instruction (CAI) uses the computer to teach: it presents lessons, drill-and-practice exercises and tests, and marks them, taking some of the teacher's role. Computer-aided learning (CAL) uses the computer as a resource that learners explore for themselves, such as encyclopaedias and tutorials. E-learning delivers lessons over the internet, so learners can study at a distance and at their own pace. Computer-based simulation models real situations, such as a science experiment or a flight, so learners can practise safely and cheaply.

### 📝 In the exam

KCSE often asks: "Explain two ways ICT is used in the banking industry", "Describe how a POS terminal helps in stock control", "Explain how a reservation system prevents double booking", or "Differentiate between CAI and CAL". Each point should name the system and say what it does, for example "ATMs allow customers to withdraw cash at any time without queueing in a banking hall". For communication, remember fax (sends a scanned copy of a document over telephone lines), email, radio and TV broadcasting, video conferencing (people in different places meet using cameras, microphones and screens over a network) and telecommuting (working from home using a computer linked to the office network).

## Mission: ICT in your town

- Next time you are at a supermarket, watch the till: count how many items are scanned by barcode and note what is printed on the receipt.
- Ask an adult how they pay bills or send money without visiting a bank. Which system do they use?
- Look at an online bus, train or airline booking page and find where it shows which seats are already taken.

Question: Name one business system you saw, and explain in one sentence what ICT does in it.

## Quiz

### Which technology do banks use to read the details printed at the bottom of a cheque?
- [x] Magnetic ink character recognition (MICR)
- [ ] Optical mark recognition (OMR)
- [ ] Point-of-sale (POS) terminal and barcode reader
- [ ] Kimball tag reader
Why: Cheque details are printed in magnetic ink, which MICR reads quickly and accurately, even if the cheque is stamped or written on.

### Sending money to a relative through M-Pesa is an example of...
- [ ] Computer-aided learning
- [x] Electronic funds transfer
- [ ] Magnetic ink cheque clearing
- [ ] Telecommuting
Why: Electronic funds transfer (EFT) moves money from one account to another electronically, without cash or cheques changing hands.

### Which task is carried out by a payroll system?
- [ ] Reading barcodes on goods and updating stock levels at the till
- [ ] Booking airline seats and printing tickets for passengers
- [x] Calculating workers' net pay after deductions and printing payslips
- [ ] Clearing cheques between banks using magnetic ink readers on each cheque
Why: Payroll systems work out gross pay, deductions such as PAYE and NSSF, and net pay for each employee, then produce payslips and reports.

### How does a point-of-sale terminal help with stock control?
- [ ] It counts the cash in the till at the end of the day and banks it automatically
- [ ] It prints adverts for items that customers have not bought recently
- [ ] It emails customers a list of items that are now on special offer
- [x] It updates the stock file as each item is sold and flags items to reorder
Why: Each sale reduces the stock level in the stock file automatically, so the shop knows when an item falls below its reorder level.

### An ATM allows a bank customer to...
- [x] Withdraw cash and check their balance at any time
- [ ] Clear the cheques they deposit at other banks' branches
- [ ] Calculate the monthly salaries of all the bank's staff
- [ ] Get their monthly payslip printed by the bank's payroll system
Why: ATMs provide 24-hour self-service banking using a card and PIN, for withdrawals, balance enquiries and mini statements.

### What is the main way a reservation system avoids double booking?
- [ ] It allows bookings only at the airport counter on the day of travel
- [x] It updates a central database in real time and locks a seat during booking
- [ ] It prints all tickets in advance and posts them to customers by mail
- [ ] It keeps a separate list of seats at each travel agent, updated once every evening
Why: Real-time processing and record locking mean a seat or room shown as available really is free, and only one terminal can book it at a time.

### What is the difference between CAI and CAL?
- [ ] CAI is used only in banks; CAL is used only in hospitals and clinics
- [ ] CAI needs an internet connection; CAL works only on offline computers
- [x] In CAI the computer teaches and tests; in CAL learners use it as a resource
- [ ] CAI is used to prepare timetables; CAL is used to keep student records and fees
Why: Computer-aided instruction takes on a teaching role with drills and tests, while computer-aided learning supports learners who direct their own study.

### Trainee pilots practise landing in bad weather on a computer before flying a real aircraft. This is...
- [ ] Electronic funds transfer (EFT)
- [ ] A payroll system
- [ ] Telecommuting
- [x] Computer-based simulation
Why: Simulation models a real situation so people can practise safely and cheaply, without risking lives or equipment.

### An accountant in Nakuru works from home and sends reports to her Nairobi office over the internet. This is called...
- [x] Telecommuting
- [ ] Video conferencing
- [ ] E-learning
- [ ] Teletext
Why: Telecommuting means working away from the office, usually at home, using computers and communication links such as the internet.

### Managers in Nairobi, Kisumu and Eldoret hold a live meeting where they see and hear each other on screens. This is...
- [ ] Facsimile (fax)
- [x] Video conferencing
- [ ] Electronic mail (email)
- [ ] Radio broadcasting
Why: Video conferencing uses cameras, microphones, screens and a network so people in different places can meet face to face, saving travel time and cost.
