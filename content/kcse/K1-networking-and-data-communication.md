# K1 · Networking & Data Communication

## Read

### 🌐 What a network is, and why we network

A computer network is a collection of independent computers and other devices linked together by a transmission medium so that they can communicate and share resources. Purposes of networking: resource sharing (data, programs and hardware such as one printer for a whole lab), remote communication (users far apart exchange messages and files, as when a bank branch in Kisumu updates records held in Nairobi), distributed processing (tasks and data are spread across several computers instead of one central machine), cost effectiveness (shared hardware and software licences cost less than buying for every user) and reliability (if one computer fails, data can be fetched from another and work continues).

### ⚠️ Limitations of networking

Networks also bring problems. Security threats: data is exposed to hackers, viruses and eavesdropping as it moves across the network. High initial cost: cables, devices, network software and trained staff are expensive to set up. Moral and cultural effects: easy access to the internet can expose users, especially the young, to pornography and other harmful material, and can erode cultural values. Over-reliance: when an organisation depends fully on its network, a breakdown stops all work, as when a bank's system goes down and no transactions can be made. Networks are classified by area as LAN (local area network: one building or campus such as a school), MAN (metropolitan area network: a town or city) and WAN (wide area network: a country or the world, the internet being the largest).

### 📶 Data communication terms

Data communication is the transmission of data from one location to another. A data signal is the electromagnetic or voltage form in which data travels. An analogue signal is a continuous wave that varies smoothly; a digital signal is discrete, made of on/off pulses (1s and 0s). Modulation converts digital data into analogue form for transmission and demodulation converts it back; a modem (modulator-demodulator) does both. Multiplexing sends several signals at the same time over one medium; a multiplexer combines them and a demultiplexer separates them at the other end. Bandwidth is the maximum amount of data a channel can carry per second, measured in bits per second (bps). Baseband carries one signal at a time using the whole bandwidth of the medium, as in most LANs; broadband carries many signals at once on different frequencies. Attenuation is the loss of signal strength as it travels further along a medium. Two other impairments spoil data on the way: noise, unwanted signals from outside (such as electrical interference) that mix with the data, and distortion, where the shape of the signal changes so the receiver may misread it. Guided (bounded) media direct signals along a physical path such as a cable; unguided (unbounded) media send signals through air or space.

### ↔️ Transmission modes, serial and parallel

Simplex: data flows in one direction only, for example radio and television broadcasts such as KBC (Kenya Broadcasting Corporation). Half duplex: data flows in both directions but only one way at a time, for example a walkie-talkie or police radio where users take turns. Full duplex: data flows in both directions at the same time, for example a telephone conversation. Serial transmission sends bits one after another along a single line; it is cheaper and reliable over long distances, so it is used in USB (Universal Serial Bus), network cables and telephone lines. Parallel transmission sends several bits at the same time along separate lines; it is faster but only suitable for short distances because bits can arrive out of step (skew), so it was used for old printer cables and inside the computer's buses.

### 📝 In the exam

KCSE often asks: define a computer network; state three purposes of networking; state two limitations of networks; differentiate between LAN and WAN; define terms such as bandwidth, attenuation, multiplexing, modulation and baseband; explain the difference between simplex, half duplex and full duplex giving an example of each; and compare serial and parallel transmission. Remember: a modem modulates (digital to analogue) and demodulates (analogue to digital); attenuation is loss of signal strength over distance; bandwidth is measured in bits per second; and always give an example with each transmission mode to earn the full marks.

## Quiz

### Which of these best defines a computer network?
- [ ] A single powerful computer that many users share by taking turns at one keyboard
- [ ] A collection of application programs installed on one computer to share its memory
- [x] Two or more computers linked by a medium so they can communicate and share resources
- [ ] A group of standalone computers kept in the same room but not connected to each other
Why: A network needs two or more devices, a transmission medium linking them, and the purpose of communicating and sharing resources such as printers and files.

### Which of the following is a limitation of networking?
- [x] Data is exposed to security threats such as hackers and viruses
- [ ] Several users can share one printer, which reduces the cost of buying printers
- [ ] Users can access shared data and files from remote locations
- [ ] Work can continue on other computers if one computer breaks down
Why: Sharing, remote access and reliability are advantages; security threats, high set-up cost, moral effects and over-reliance on the network are limitations.

### A network linking the branches of a bank in Nairobi, Mombasa and Eldoret is best described as a...
- [ ] LAN
- [ ] PAN
- [ ] MAN
- [x] WAN
Why: A WAN covers a large geographical area such as a whole country; a MAN covers a single town or city and a LAN covers a building or campus.

### What is modulation?
- [ ] Combining several signals so that they share one channel at the same time
- [x] Converting digital data into an analogue signal for transmission
- [ ] The gradual loss of signal strength as it travels along the medium
- [ ] Converting an analogue signal back into digital data at the receiver
Why: Modulation turns digital data into analogue form so it can travel over lines such as telephone lines; demodulation does the reverse. A modem does both.

### Multiplexing is...
- [ ] Boosting a weak signal so that it can travel a longer distance
- [ ] Converting digital data into analogue signals for the telephone line
- [ ] Sending data in one direction only, from the sender to the receiver
- [x] Sending several signals at the same time over one medium
Why: A multiplexer combines many signals on one line, and a demultiplexer separates them at the receiving end, so one expensive medium serves many users.

### Bandwidth is measured in...
- [x] Bits per second (bps)
- [ ] Dots per inch (dpi)
- [ ] Metres per second (m/s)
- [ ] Pages per minute (ppm)
Why: Bandwidth is the maximum amount of data a channel can carry per second, given in bps, kbps, Mbps or Gbps. Dpi measures print resolution and ppm printer speed.

### Which statement about baseband transmission is correct?
- [ ] It divides the medium into many channels, each on a different frequency
- [x] It uses the whole bandwidth of the medium for one signal at a time
- [ ] It is used only for links between earth stations and satellites
- [ ] It sends signals through the air instead of along a cable
Why: Baseband uses the full capacity of the medium for one signal, as in most LANs; broadband splits the medium into many channels on different frequencies.

### A walkie-talkie lets both users speak, but only one at a time. This mode is...
- [ ] Simplex
- [ ] Full duplex
- [x] Half duplex
- [ ] Parallel
Why: Half duplex allows two-way communication, but in only one direction at any one time; simplex is one way only and full duplex is both ways at once.

### Why is parallel transmission not used over long distances?
- [ ] It sends only one bit at a time along a single line, so it is too slow
- [ ] It can carry only analogue signals, not the digital data that computers use
- [ ] It is slower than serial transmission over every distance and every medium
- [x] Bits sent together can arrive out of step, and many lines are costly
Why: Parallel is fast over short distances, but over long cables the bits drift out of step (skew) and the many wires cost more, so serial is used instead.

### Which of these uses guided (bounded) media?
- [ ] A school's satellite TV link to an orbiting satellite
- [ ] A radio station broadcasting to its listeners
- [x] A computer connected by a network cable
- [ ] A Bluetooth headset paired with a phone
Why: Guided media carry signals along a physical path such as a cable; unguided media such as satellite, radio and Bluetooth send signals through air or space.
