# K5 · ICT in Industry, Science & Society

## Read

### 🏭 Industry

Industrial simulation models a system, such as a new production line or a nuclear plant, on a computer so designers can test it before building. Process control uses sensors to monitor a continuous process, such as temperature in a brewery or pressure in an oil refinery; the computer compares readings with set values and adjusts valves or heaters automatically. Computer-aided design (CAD) lets engineers and architects draw and test products and buildings on screen, and computer-aided manufacturing (CAM) uses those designs to control machines that cut and shape parts. Robots do repetitive, heavy or dangerous jobs, such as welding and painting car bodies, accurately and without tiring.

### 🔬 Science, research and libraries

Weather forecasting uses powerful computers to process huge amounts of data from weather stations, satellites and radar, run models and predict the weather. In medical research computers analyse test results, model diseases and help design drugs; in hospitals expert systems help diagnose illness. The military uses ICT for simulating battles, guiding missiles and keeping secure communication. Library systems record the lending of books (who borrowed what and when it is due), keep an inventory of stock, and hold a computerised catalogue so users can search by title, author or subject.

### 🚆 Transport, entertainment and the home

Air traffic control uses radar and computers to track aircraft and keep them safely apart during take-off, flight and landing. Shipping and railway control systems track vessels and trains and control signals and points. GPS (global positioning system) uses satellites to find the exact position of a vehicle, so companies can track lorries, buses and parcels. In entertainment, computers are used to play and create games, record and edit music, and edit video and make special effects. At home people use ICT for study, budgeting, communication and entertainment, and to control appliances.

### 🛡️ Expert systems, marketing, VR and law enforcement

An expert system copies the knowledge of a human expert. It has three parts: a knowledge base (facts and rules from experts), an inference engine (which applies the rules to the user's answers to reach a conclusion) and a user interface (through which the user asks and answers questions). In marketing, ICT supports e-commerce (buying and selling online), electronic presentations to clients, and advertising on websites and social media. Virtual reality (VR) creates a 3D artificial world that the user enters with a head-mounted display (HMD) and data gloves that sense hand movements. Law enforcement uses biometrics such as fingerprint matching to identify suspects, CCTV cameras to monitor public places, and computerised police records to store and search criminal records quickly.

### 📝 In the exam

KCSE often asks: "Explain three ways computers are used in industry", "Name the three components of an expert system and state the function of each", "Explain how ICT is used in weather forecasting", "State two devices used to interact with a virtual reality system", or "Describe how computers are used in law enforcement". Remember: process control uses sensors and works in real time; CAD is for designing, CAM is for manufacturing; an expert system is knowledge base + inference engine + user interface; VR uses a head-mounted display and data gloves. Always link the device or system to what it does.

### Task: pick

#### A milk processing plant in Nakuru must keep pasteurising tanks at a fixed temperature. Sensors feed readings to a computer that switches heaters on and off. This is...
- [ ] Computer-aided design
- [x] Process control
- [ ] Virtual reality
- [ ] E-commerce
Why: Process control monitors a continuous process with sensors and makes adjustments automatically.

#### A doctor answers questions on screen and the program suggests a likely illness by applying stored rules. Which part of the system applies the rules?
- [ ] The user interface
- [ ] The knowledge base
- [x] The inference engine
- [ ] The printer
Why: The knowledge base stores the facts and rules; the inference engine reasons with them to reach a conclusion.

#### A transport company wants to know where each of its lorries is on the Mombasa–Nairobi highway at any time. What should it use?
- [x] GPS tracking
- [ ] CAD software
- [ ] A library catalogue
- [ ] Data gloves
Why: GPS uses satellite signals to find each vehicle's exact position, which can be sent back to the company.

## Quiz

### Which of the following is the best example of process control?
- [ ] An architect drawing a house plan on screen
- [ ] A librarian searching the catalogue
- [x] A computer using sensors to keep the temperature in an oil refinery within set limits
- [ ] A shopper buying shoes online
Why: Process control uses sensors to monitor a continuous process and automatically adjusts it to keep it within set values.

### What does CAM stand for, and what does it do?
- [x] Computer-aided manufacturing: computers control the machines that make products
- [ ] Computer-aided marketing: computers advertise products
- [ ] Computer-assisted mail: computers send letters
- [ ] Computer-aided memory: computers store more data
Why: CAD is used to design a product, and CAM uses the design to control machine tools that make it.

### Why are robots used on car assembly lines?
- [ ] They need no electricity
- [ ] They can make their own decisions about car designs
- [ ] They are cheaper to buy than any human worker's yearly wage
- [x] They do repetitive, heavy or dangerous tasks accurately and without tiring
Why: Robots are ideal for welding, painting and lifting, where precision and safety matter.

### Which are the three components of an expert system?
- [ ] Input, process and output
- [x] Knowledge base, inference engine and user interface
- [ ] Monitor, keyboard and mouse
- [ ] Sensor, actuator and robot
Why: The knowledge base holds expert facts and rules, the inference engine reasons with them, and the user interface lets the user interact.

### How do computers help in weather forecasting?
- [ ] They control the rain
- [ ] They replace weather stations completely
- [ ] They only print weather maps
- [x] They process large amounts of data from satellites and weather stations to predict the weather
Why: Weather forecasting needs fast computers to analyse huge volumes of data and run models of the atmosphere.

### Which two devices are commonly used to interact with a virtual reality system?
- [x] Head-mounted display and data gloves
- [ ] Barcode reader and POS terminal
- [ ] MICR reader and printer
- [ ] Plotter and scanner
Why: The head-mounted display shows the 3D world and tracks head movement, and data gloves sense hand movement.

### Which task does a computerised library system perform?
- [ ] Controlling aircraft landings
- [x] Recording which member borrowed a book and when it is due back
- [ ] Welding metal parts
- [ ] Forecasting rainfall
Why: Library systems handle lending, keep an inventory of books and provide a searchable catalogue.

### How does ICT help air traffic control?
- [ ] It sells airline tickets
- [ ] It designs new aircraft engines
- [x] It uses radar and computers to track aircraft and keep them safely apart
- [ ] It prints boarding passes
Why: Air traffic control systems show controllers each aircraft's position and height so they can prevent collisions.

### The police compare a fingerprint found at a crime scene with millions of stored records in seconds. This is an example of...
- [ ] Computer-aided design
- [ ] E-commerce
- [ ] Process control
- [x] Biometric identification
Why: Biometrics use unique body features, such as fingerprints, to identify people, and computers can search large records quickly.

### A Kenyan craft business sells baskets to customers through its website and receives payment online. This is...
- [x] E-commerce
- [ ] Telecommuting
- [ ] Simulation
- [ ] Process control
Why: E-commerce is buying and selling goods and services over the internet, reaching customers far beyond the shop.
